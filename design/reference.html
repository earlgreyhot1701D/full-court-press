The story so far

The Lakers won 53 games, took the 4 seed, and got swept out of the playoffs by Oklahoma City. Then the real season started. LeBron James declined to re-sign after 23 years. Deandre Ayton was traded to Washington for a bench guard and picks. In one 35-minute stretch of free agency, the front office signed four new players — a center coming off shoulder surgery, a 3&D wing coming off the worst shooting year of his career, a bench bucket-getter, and a stretch big off a career season.

Every one of those bets rides on one player: Luka Dončić, who just led the NBA in usage and scoring. The roster just hit 15 of 15 — and the front office is reportedly still shopping for a bigger move anyway. So this page does what the hot-take machine won't: it scores every arrival, every returnee, and every affordable name still on the market against Luka's measurable needs, with the assumptions on the table and the receipts one scroll away.

The rules we scored by

Decided before the scores, so the scores couldn't bend them. The tool keeps a public record of correcting itself.
WHY USAGE?

The whole thesis

Usage rate is the share of team possessions a player ends — a shot, free throws, or a turnover. It's the cleanest measurable answer to "who has the ball?" Luka ends more possessions than anyone alive, so every teammate's fit starts with one question: can you be great without it?

NEVER

No blended score

One composite number implies a verdict the data can't back. Four axes stay four axes. If you want a single number, this is the wrong tool on purpose.

AXES

Four measurable needs

Spacing (3P% + volume), play finishing (TS% without the ball), defensive cover (who guards so Luka doesn't), and ball-need — inverted: lower usage scores higher next to the league's highest-usage player.

MUST

Confidence tags are first-class

Sample size, team context, and injury status sit next to every score, not in a footnote. Tags changed the verdict on 2 of the first 4 players scored.

FOUND

The Luka rest problem

Season usage blends minutes next to Luka with minutes while he rests — opposite signals. Fix: with/without splits. Applying it softened the tool's own loudest take (see Reaves).

UPGRADED

Defense, second pass

Cover scores originally leaned on steals, blocks, and reputation. They're now rescored on individual defensive rating — points allowed per 100 possessions on court — pulled for all 14 scored players. Still box-derived and team-flavored; the tracking-data tier stays stubbed and the skeleton stays visible.

LENS

Fit and need never merge

Fit asks "does his game complement Luka." Need asks "does this roster have room for his body type" — with 7 guards, 4 forwards, and 2.5 centers after the July signings, need is countable, not a vibe. Two lenses, shown separately.

How to read more gold = stronger fit vs Luka's needs (0–5) purple = fit flag Ball-need inverted: lower usage = more gold

Ch. 4 · The cap

The cap sheet is the invisible fifth axis: it decided who the Lakers could even ask about. Figures are reported numbers, not team-confirmed, via the sources in the next section.
THE ROOM

~$53M in cap space

LeBron declining to re-sign is the quiet enabler of the whole offseason. His departure, plus the Ayton trade shedding salary, turned the Lakers into a rare cap-room team — and they spent essentially all of it in one July week.

THE CEILING

Hard-capped at the first apron

Using cap room hard-capped the Lakers at roughly $209.1M for 15 players. That ceiling, not preference, is why every signing after Sexton came at the veteran minimum — and why the FA board on this page only shopped the minimum aisle.

THE LAST DOLLAR

Room exception → Sexton

The $9.4M room exception was the final real spending tool, and most of it went to Sexton's two-year, $19M deal. After that: minimums only. Looney and Ziaire Williams both arrived on them.

THE DISCOUNT

Reaves took $5M less

Re-signed Jul 13 at four years, $180M with a 2029-30 player option — down from the $185M max he'd already agreed to. The reported reason: preserving the Lakers' path to the non-taxpayer midlevel exception next summer. The co-star the ball-need axis once flagged just paid for the team's next signing himself.
